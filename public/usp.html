The everyday mess

Everything's somewhere. Finding it is the problem.

Files pile up across folders, drives and repos. The tools around them weren't built for how people actually look for things — or for handing one piece to someone else without handing over everything.

You remember the file, not its name

Search by filename only works if you named it well a year ago. Nobody did. "The red logo" and "last quarter's invoice" draw a blank.

Sharing means over-sharing

Send a cloud link and the recipient can wander the whole drive — or you spend ten minutes copying files into a new folder just to share three of them.

Your work lives on someone's cloud

Client files, contracts, raw footage — all sitting on a service you don't control, priced per seat, indexed by machines you'll never see.

How it works

Point it at your folders. That's the setup.

No migration, no re-uploading, no reorganizing. SourceBox reads what you already have and makes it findable and shareable in minutes.

01 — Point

Add a folder or repo

Give it a path on your computer, or paste a git URL to clone. Files stay exactly where they are — nothing is duplicated. A watcher picks up every change on its own.

C:\Work\Clients  ·  git@…/brand.git
02 — Find

Describe what you want

Type the way you'd say it. SourceBox understands synonyms, short forms and typos across any kind of file, then previews images, PDFs, video, docs and more right in the browser.

"signed contract pdf"  ·  "product photos"
03 — Share

Hand over one folder

Create a link to a single folder with its own passcode, an expiry date, and downloads on or off. The recipient sees that folder and nothing else. Revoke it any time.

one folder · passcode · expires in 7 days

Private by construction

The only copy of your files is the one you already have.

SourceBox runs as a small server on your own machine. There's no cloud to sign into and nothing phones home — your account is a row in a file on your own disk, not a record in someone else's database. Share over your local Wi-Fi, or open a temporary public link only when you choose to.

  • Your account, on your disk

    An email and a salted hash in data/users.json. No vendor, no verification email, no third party.

  • Nothing leaves unasked

    No telemetry, no analytics, no background uploads. Files are served from disk, to you.

  • You hold the keys

    Owner passcode for the vault; separate passcodes, expiries and revocation for every shared link.

Sign in

One account each, instead of one shared secret.

Everyone who works out of the vault gets their own email and password. It stays entirely local: the accounts live in a file next to your projects, and nothing is sent anywhere to create one.

  • The first account owns the vault

    Whoever signs up first on a fresh install becomes the owner. Nothing else to configure.

  • After that, the vault passcode is the invitation

    A second account can only be created by someone who knows the owner passcode, so a vault reachable over Wi-Fi or a public link cannot be signed into by a stranger.

  • Passwords are never stored

    Only a scrypt hash with a per-account salt. Sign-in attempts are rate limited, and a session is a signed, http-only cookie that lasts 30 days.

  • The passcode still works

    Accounts sit on top of the owner passcode, they do not replace it. You can still unlock the vault with the passcode alone, which is handy on a phone or if a password is forgotten.
